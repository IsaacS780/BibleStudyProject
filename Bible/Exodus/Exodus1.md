# Exodus Chapter 1
Exodus 1 serves as the crucial bridge between the patriarchal family of Genesis and the birth of the nation of Israel. The chapter opens by listing the sons of Jacob, highlighting that despite the deaths of Joseph and his generation, God remains faithful to His covenant promise to Abraham by multiplying the Israelites exceedingly in Egypt. Threatened by their growth, a new Pharaoh, who did not know Joseph, subjects the Hebrews to brutal forced labor in an attempt to crush their spirit and limit their numbers. When oppression fails to diminish them, Pharaoh escalates his cruelty by ordering the Hebrew midwives, Shiphrah and Puah, to kill all newborn boys. Fearing God more than the king, the midwives courageously disobey, choosing civil disobedience to preserve life, for which God blesses them with families of their own. Frustrated, Pharaoh commands all his people to cast every newborn Hebrew boy into the Nile River, setting the stage for the arrival of Israel's deliverer. 

For personal reflection and study, Exodus 1 demonstrates the clash between the kingdom of man and the kingdom of God. It teaches that human oppression cannot thwart God's sovereign plans and covenant promises. The midwives' courageous actions serve as a timeless example of godly fear triumphing over the fear of man, challenging believers to remain faithful to God’s moral law even under hostile authorities.
