# Exodus Chapter 2
Exodus 2 chronicles the birth, exile, and preparation of Israel’s deliverer, Moses, highlighting God’s sovereign providence and covenant faithfulness. Born under Pharaoh's decree of death, Moses is miraculously preserved through the faith of his mother and the intervention of Pharaoh’s daughter, showing how God can use even the enemy’s household to protect His chosen instrument. As an adult, Moses attempts to deliver his brethren through human strength by killing an Egyptian taskmaster, which backfires and forces him to flee to the wilderness of Midian. In this 40-year period of obscurity, Moses marries Zipporah, becomes a shepherd, and undergoes the character-shaping necessary for his ultimate calling. The chapter closes with the Israelites groaning in their bitter slavery, and God hearing, seeing, and remembering His covenant with Abraham, Isaac, and Jacob. For reflection, this chapter teaches that God is actively working behind the scenes during periods of silent suffering, preparing His servants and remaining utterly faithful to His promises.
