# John Chapter 1
John chapter 1 introduces the profound identity and mission of Jesus Christ, transitioning from eternity past to His earthly ministry. The chapter opens with a majestic prologue establishing Jesus as "the Word" (Logos)—fully divine, co-eternal with the Father, and the active agent in creation. In a pivotal theological declaration, the text reveals the Incarnation: the eternal God became human flesh to dwell among humanity, bringing grace and truth. The narrative then shifts to the historical testimony of John the Baptist, who humbles himself as a mere voice in the wilderness preparing the way for Christ, whom he identifies as the "Lamb of God who takes away the sin of the world." The chapter concludes with Jesus calling His first disciples—Andrew, Peter, Philip, and Nathanael. Through these personal encounters, the disciples begin to recognize Jesus as the Messiah, the Son of God, and the ultimate bridge between heaven and earth. For study, this chapter anchors the Christian faith in the deity of Christ, highlights the power of personal testimony, and extends a universal invitation to "come and see" the Savior.
